# Subject access request — police

**Send to:** the Data Protection / Information Rights unit of the force that holds the
data. Given Oxford and Cowley in your diary, **Thames Valley Police** is the likely one,
but send to whichever force you actually dealt with — forces do not share SAR handling.

**Most forces have an online SAR form.** Use it if offered: it routes straight to the
right unit and starts the clock immediately. Paste the body of this letter into the free
-text field.

**This is not the same as a basic disclosure.** If what you actually need is a criminal
record certificate for an employer, landlord or visa, that is **Disclosure and Barring
Service** (dbs.gov.uk) or a **police certificate** (ACRO), not a SAR — those produce a
formal certificate, and a SAR does not. A SAR gets you what they hold about you.

```
2. Local force records — incident logs, crime reports, custody records, stop and

3. Any records in which I appear as a victim, witness or reporting person, as

4. The supplementary information required by Article 15(1): the purposes of
   processing, the categories of data, the recipients or categories of recipient
   to whom my data has been disclosed, the retention period or the criteria used
   to set it, and the source of any data not obtained from me.

If any part is withheld under an exemption in Schedule 2 of the Data Protection

I understand this request is free and must be answered within one calendar month
of receipt, or of receipt of my identification if required. I enclose [DESCRIBE
ID] to avoid delay.

Yours faithfully,

[SIGN]
[YOUR FULL NAME]
```

## Notes before you send

**Expect some of this to be withheld, lawfully.** Schedule 2 Part 1 of the DPA 2018
exempts personal data where disclosure would be likely to prejudice the prevention or
detection of crime, or the apprehension or prosecution of offenders. Intelligence-held
material is frequently withheld on that basis. **They should still tell you they have
withheld something**, and that is the part worth pushing on if the response is silent.

**Previous addresses matter more here than elsewhere.** Force records are often indexed
geographically, so an incomplete address history is a common reason a search comes back
thinner than it should.

**Ask for the victim/witness records explicitly** — point 3. Some forces read a bare
request as suspect-records-only, and you may well appear in reports you made rather than
reports about you.

**Send it with proof of delivery.** For this one especially: the deadline runs from
receipt, and being able to evidence the date is what makes an ICO complaint
straightforward later.
