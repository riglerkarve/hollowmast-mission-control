# Opus 5 Ultra — shift handover

One task this shift, taken directly from the owner: *"create a fun mini game."*

## Built

**SHUNT** — a third game in `Mini Games/`, one self-contained HTML file, no dependencies,
no external URL. `shunt.html`, 56.7 KB, 986 lines of JS. Published as a private artifact:
`https://claude.ai/code/artifact/8b482c5a-c659-4703-ae3a-caad8a4d466f`.

Crates burst out of charging hoppers onto a scrolling floor; you tap 45-degree pins to steer
each colour into its own bay. Pay is columns crossed, so the money is in long diagonals — and
a long diagonal is a **colour-blind wall** every other stream now has to cross. Pin stock sits
deliberately below a static solve, so part of every route is hand-played with a free paddle
held under the finger.

**Why not a third timing game.** GIVE WAY and PEAL are the same species: one input, one thing
to read, commit at the right instant, nothing persists between beats. SHUNT is the one where
the player *leaves something behind*. That is the axis to check a fourth game against.

Design was chosen by a nine-agent panel (five concepts under different lenses, three judges on
different criteria, one synthesis) — ultracode was on for this session. Two of the five
concepts were rejected specifically as re-treads of the two games already on the shelf.

## Verified

`Mini Games/tools/shunt-harness.cjs` runs the **shipped file** in a Node vm and plays three
arms through the real `press()`/`release()` path. **37/37 pass against the committed bytes**
(HEAD and working tree both sha1 `dddc028`).

- **It caught a launch blocker on its first real run.** `syncHud()` walks the grid at load,
  before `beginRun()` ever built one — it threw before `requestAnimationFrame`, leaving a
  title card whose Start button did nothing. That would have shipped.
- `traceRoute()` is the single source of truth for where a crate lands, and the on-screen
  route ghost is drawn from it. **3,200 drops over 400 random boards, 0 disagreements.**
- Three arms, same input path: **passive 111 points, dead at 27s; greedy-without-checking 48,
  dead at 12s; reading the ghost 3,141, alive at 80s.** Greed without reading the floor is
  worse than doing nothing — that spread is the evidence the skill ceiling is real.
- Contrast via the committed `dash/contrast-audit.js` (not a second oracle): **0 failures**
  across title, play and game-over, 20 text roles, none on a gradient.
- In-browser at 1280x720 and 375x812: board fits, HUD clears it, hold-to-paddle conversion and
  the stock refund both fire through the real `step()`, clean-burst bundle pays as specified.

## Risks

- **Nobody has played it.** Every balance number is from bot arms. The arms prove the skill
  ceiling *exists* and that the loop is survivable and scoreable; they cannot tell you whether
  it is fun in the hand. If it plays wrong, the four dials in order are `PIN_PER_HOPPER` (3.0,
  tuned to 75% of the mean far-route cost — an invariant, not taste), `RATE_TARGET`,
  `PRESS_HOLD_MS` (200 ms) and `FALL_0`.
- **Two constants are load-bearing and easy to destroy by accident.** Both opening hoppers sit
  over a bay of their own colour, so a player who touches nothing loses nothing while working
  out what they are looking at; moving one reintroduces an opening that costs a new player
  three of five lamps in six seconds. And a crate's slide must take exactly one row-time or
  the diagonals stop reading as 45 degrees and the board becomes unreadable.
- **GIVE WAY and PEAL declare no charset and mojibake when served as bare `text/html`.**
  Latent, not live: they are played as artifacts, where the host supplies the head. Found
  because it bit SHUNT on my own preview. SHUNT now declares a charset *and* escapes every
  user-visible non-ASCII character. I did not touch the other two — committed, published,
  parked, and not my call.

## What I learned

The supervisor's sentence — *a check you ran yourself is the hardest evidence to read
honestly* — landed twice in one afternoon, both times as a **pass**:

- My pin-scarcity check ran on state left dirty by the previous test, so it measured 8/10/13
  instead of 9/12/14, and I had written the band loose enough that the wrong numbers passed.
  Two errors that cancelled into a green tick.
- My "greed pays better than safety" check compared a *bad* greedy bot against a *safe* one.
  The safe one won, the check failed, and the failure was the useful part: it proved nothing
  about skilled greed. Adding a third arm that verifies its own board with the on-screen ghost
  turned a meaningless comparison into the strongest evidence in the file.

Both were checks that agreed with themselves. The tell in each case was a number that looked
*fine* — 80%, and a safe bot outscoring a greedy one — rather than a number that looked wrong.
