## 4. The rules, and why each exists

1. **Commit by pathspec, never a bare `git commit`.** Nine sessions share one `.git/index`. On 19 August that hazard fired three times in one hour; once it would have rolled the public game backwards under another session's name. `git commit <paths>` cannot take another session's staging. It fails on untracked files, and one bad path rejects the whole commit.
2. **Check that *your* path left the index. Never that the index is empty.** Another session's staged files legitimately remain. Unstaging what you did not stage is the destructive version of tidying.
3. **Write the payload to a file.** Three separate manglings on 19 August — an apostrophe closing a `-m` string, a hook with no shebang, backticks run as command substitution inside `node -e`. All silent, all left plausible artefacts. `git commit -F`, and `node script.cjs` rather than `node -e`.
4. **Verify the control is a control.** A hook that cannot run passes both arms of its own test.
5. **Measure the artefact you ship.** `origin/main` is a model of what the public gets. Between them sits a deploy.
6. **A check you ran yourself is the hardest to read honestly**, because you already know what it was supposed to say. Five instances across three sessions in one day.
7. **File it, do not flag it.** A finding in a message dies when the message scrolls.
8. ~~**Codex owns every stylesheet in `mission-control`.**~~ **REVERSED 23 Aug 2026 — decision #48 supersedes #18.** The owner took CSS control back: *"Take back controll of css from codex"*. Claude sessions may edit stylesheets here again. **What has not changed is the risk #18 named**, which was overruled rather than refuted — four agents editing seven stylesheets in one afternoon, in a shared working tree where a whole-file commit from a stale copy silently reverts another session's work. So: **scope your edits to your own panel's sheet**, and treat `shared.css` and `shell.css` like a migration, because they reach all 66 panels at once. The watch condition is now *two sessions committing to the same stylesheet in one shift*, or a panel losing styling it had yesterday.
